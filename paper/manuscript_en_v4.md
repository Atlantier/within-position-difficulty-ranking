# Ranking Programming Problems Within Contest Positions Under a Limited Review Budget

## Abstract

When maintainers curate adjacent-contest problems sharing the same position under a fixed chronological manual review budget, does reducing automated misorderings stem from stronger ranking or better referral selection? We formulate this workflow as a retrospective decision simulation using release-visible statements alone across historical Codeforces and AtCoder contests, decoupling ranking from margin-based referral via counterfactual swapping and same-ranker random-referral baselines. At a fixed 30% referral budget on AtCoder ABC (193 of 646 pairs referred; 453 automated), DeepSeek-Flash ordering left 119 verifiable errors (26.27% automated error rate; −14.55 per 100 requests versus joint ridge's 213), compared with 141 under ridge referral and 142.7 expected under random referral (fixed-ranker margin gain +3.67 per 100, 95% CI +2.06 to +5.38). Locally adapted Qwen3-A0 left 168 errors (three seeds: 168, 170, 180; margin gain +0.78, −1.13 to +2.61). On 400 Codeforces Div. 2 requests, joint ridge and DeepSeek-Flash left 82 and 87 errors (80 and 93 official ties), with uncertain margin gains across both Codeforces cohorts. In a Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B factorial, a training-only position offset yielded no stable within-position gain on Codeforces Div. 2 (−1.09 percentage points, −6.09 to +4.03), whereas un-offset LoRA improved ABC accuracy over frozen probing (+5.73 points, +1.85 to +9.66).

**Keywords:** competitive programming problems, difficulty ranking, selective referral, review budget, low-rank adaptation, retrospective decision simulation

## 1. Introduction and Related Work

Consider two adjacent Beginner Contests on AtCoder: ABC319 B asks contestants to print a divisibility ruler string, whereas ABC320 B asks for the length of the longest palindromic substring. Because both problems occupy position B in consecutive rounds of the same series, contest position provides no ordering signal. Archived community ratings assign them −299 and −225, respectively, identifying ABC320 B as harder. Yet a lexical-and-surface ridge ranker predicts the reverse direction (1,467 vs. 1,056), while DeepSeek-Flash assigns single-item scores of 5 and 10 and an adapted local neural ranker (Qwen3-A0) scores them −1.99 and −1.70—both agreeing with community difficulty. Chronologically, ABC319 B versus ABC320 B is the earliest AtCoder ABC position-B pair where joint ridge errs and DeepSeek-Flash succeeds; while illustrative as a single pair, all aggregate claims in this study average over every eligible same-position contest block.

This opening pair reflects a core evaluation and operational dilemma. Offline benchmarks that pool comparisons across unequal contest slots allow a zero-training alphabetic position rule to reach 91.01%–93.43% accuracy while offering zero discrimination within any fixed contest position (50.00%). Once a maintainer restricts attention to adjacent same-position comparisons, statement-only rankers remain fallible. Moreover, an automated ranker that is more accurate overall is not automatically better at identifying which pairs it is most likely to misorder. Under a fixed manual review budget, the maintainer's workflow therefore splits into two distinct, separately testable decisions: *whose scores should order the automated pairs*, and *whose score margins should select which pairs are referred for manual review*.

To evaluate both decisions without relying on post-contest solve logs or contest metadata during inference, we investigate two research questions:

- **RQ1 (Within-position discrimination and training-only position offset).** Among adjacent-contest same-position pairs with unequal platform-published difficulty, does adapting a modern embedding backbone (`Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B`) with low-rank adaptation (LoRA) and a *training-only position offset* $b_k$ improve statement-only within-position accuracy relative to un-offset LoRA ($A_1 - A_0$), frozen probing ($F_0, F_1$), lexical-and-surface baselines, and an external generative judge (`DeepSeek-Flash`) across platforms?
- **RQ2 (Separating ranking from referral selection under a fixed chronological budget).** Under an online chronological manual review budget (fixed 30% primary scenario and 0%–50% curves), how much error reduction comes from switching the automated ranker versus switching the referral rule, and does margin-based referral reduce verifiable automated errors beyond uniform random referral when the automated ranker is held fixed?

Prior work on programming problem difficulty falls into two main streams. First, release-time statement models predict algorithm tags or single-item difficulty tiers directly from problem text—including Problem-Solving Guide on Codeforces [1], zero-shot or hybrid LLM difficulty judges on LeetCode [2], and multi-level student simulation via zero-shot prompting [9]. Second, online-judge and educational data-mining studies infer difficulty parameters or instructional sequences from post-contest submission traces and item response theory (IRT) histories [3–8, 22]. Unlike both streams, our setting operates strictly on release-visible problem statements and evaluates *pairwise* decisions across adjacent same-position contest blocks rather than independent single-item tiers or post-contest learner logs (a detailed literature comparison appears in Supplement S1).

Our evaluation design also builds on three methodological literatures. In recommender systems, Krichene and Rendle [10] showed that candidate-set sampling distorts offline ranking metrics and can invert model orderings; contest positions induce an analogous stratification where pooled accuracy masks within-position discrimination. In selective prediction and deferral, Geifman and El-Yaniv [23] formalized coverage–risk trade-offs for abstaining classifiers, Cheng et al. [26] studied ranking with abstention on uncertain pairwise comparisons, and Mozannar and Sontag [27] analyzed learning to defer to an expert. Relative to those frameworks, our empirical contribution isolates three operational features of problem-bank curation: naturally formed adjacent same-position requests, chronological per-block review quotas as contests arrive, and counterfactual swapping of referral selection against automated ranking on the exact same requests and budgets. Because we do not measure live editorial speed or post-review human accuracy, our study is framed as an offline retrospective decision simulation; live human-in-the-loop workflow trials remain separate future work.

## 2. Data and Methods

### 2.1 Data Sources and Evaluation Cohorts

Historical contests were eligible through 6 October 2026. All directional comparisons use the within-platform published difficulty order: official problem ratings on Codeforces [13] and unclipped community integer difficulty estimates from AtCoder Problems [14] (retaining finite negative ratings on beginner problems so early slots A and B are not discarded). Mutually exclusive archive filtering retained 3,960 Codeforces Regular and Educational Div. 2 problems and 3,598 AtCoder problems (2,705 ABC and 893 ARC), with zero exact statement overlap between training and evaluation splits.

Table 1 summarizes the five core historical cohorts plus two auxiliary boundary cohorts (AtCoder ARC format boundary and a small post-cutoff ABC temporal pilot). Within each platform, series, year, and calendar quarter, contests are sorted chronologically and paired into disjoint adjacent contest-pair blocks. Every pair of problems sharing the same letter slot across the two paired contests constitutes one *raw within-position request*. On Codeforces, official rating ties (driven largely by the 800-rating floor on position A) are unverifiable for directional accuracy and are reported separately from directional pairs. For the original cross-series Codeforces analysis (Div. 3, Div. 4, and Div. 1+2 Combined), we locked predictions before merging previously archived ratings and repeated the analysis after repairing statement preprocessing. The post-cutoff ABC pilot spans three adjacent blocks (six rounds, 21 directional same-position pairs); 35 of its 42 problems already carried identical integer ratings in a prior historical snapshot before final label merge. Complete cohort timelines and audit records appear in Supplement S7.

### 2.2 Statement Preprocessing and Evaluation Metrics

An audit of earlier statement extraction uncovered a regex defect that stripped literal `<` and `>` inequalities after HTML entity unescaping. Confining HTML tag removal strictly to the raw DOM layer before entity decoding repaired 79 of 8,502 archived statements (0 in E1 and E2) without changing any duplicate flags. Across the primary refitted contrasts, the largest absolute shift was 0.65 percentage points (1.87 points across all 105 audited comparisons), and the primary Codeforces Div. 2 lexical-over-surface increment remained unchanged at +2.19 points (Supplement S1). All learned and generative rankers evaluated in this paper take the repaired, readable release-visible statement text as input; contest IDs, problem slots, tags, editorials, and post-contest logs are strictly excluded at inference time.

For any pair $(i, j)$ with unequal platform-published ratings, directional pairwise accuracy scores a correctly ordered prediction as 1, an inverted prediction as 0, and an exact score tie ($|s_i - s_j| \le 10^{-12}$) as 0.5. All uncertainty intervals are 95% percentile confidence intervals from $B = 2,000$ paired block-bootstrap resamples over contest-pair blocks [16], preserving within-contest correlation. Note that for trained neural checkpoints, these block intervals quantify contest-sampling uncertainty conditional on a single trained model and do not include across-seed initialization variance, which we report separately. To explain why pooled evaluation diverges from within-position evaluation, note the exact identity linking pooled accuracy $A_{\mathrm{pool}}$ and within-position accuracy $A_{\mathrm{same}}$ via the within-position pair fraction $w_{\mathrm{same}}$: $A_{\mathrm{pool}} - A_{\mathrm{same}} = (1 - w_{\mathrm{same}})(A_{\mathrm{diff}} - A_{\mathrm{same}})$. Consequently, pooled benchmarks weight different-position pairs by $1 - w_{\mathrm{same}} \approx 86\%\text{–}91\%$ across our three main cohorts, allowing cross-slot separation to dominate aggregate scores (full reference-shift tables appear in Supplement S2).

### 2.3 Rankers and Controlled Four-Cell Adaptation Design

We compare four statement-only model families alongside a zero-training contest-position diagnostic baseline:

1. **Zero-training Position diagnostic baseline:** Reads the contest slot letter directly (e.g., B harder than A) solely as a structural diagnostic—not a statement-only ranker—yielding exact ties on every same-position pair (50.00% credit).
2. **Surface, Lexical, and Pairwise Linear Rankers:** Joint ridge regression [15] combining 11 release-time surface features (length, formula/digit density, constraint bounds) with word unigram and bigram TF-IDF features (`Joint Ridge`), alongside pairwise logistic RankNet [11, 12] trained on the same features (`PW-Joint`). Reference frozen encoders (`google-bert/bert-base-uncased` [17], `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5` Mean and CLS [19]) are summarized in Supplement S2.
3. **Controlled Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 2×2 Factorial (`F0, F1, A0, A1`):** To isolate representation adaptation from a *training-only position offset* on a single modern 0.6B-parameter embedding backbone [24], we train four cells on the exact 5,193 directional pairs (800 same-slot + 4,393 different-slot across 277 blocks) of Codeforces Div. 2 Train using head-plus-tail tokenization (768-token window retaining the final 256 constraint tokens; $\le 2.0\%$ truncation across all cohorts) and an L2-normalized last-token representation feeding a scalar head $f(x)$. Cells `F0` and `F1` freeze the backbone and train only the linear head; cells `A0` and `A1` apply rank-8 LoRA adapters [25] ($\alpha=16$) to attention `q_proj` and `v_proj` layers. Without the position offset (`F0, A0`), training minimizes pairwise logistic loss on $f(x_i) - f(x_j)$. With the *training-only position offset* (`F1, A1`), training minimizes pairwise logistic loss on $f(x_i) - f(x_j) + (b_{k_i} - b_{k_j})$ plus L2 regularization $\lambda_b \sum_{k \neq A} b_k^2$ ($\lambda_b = 10^{-3}$, anchored at $b_A \equiv 0$). At inference time, $b_k$ is discarded so all four cells predict strictly via $f(x)$ from statements alone. Every cell trains for five epochs under identical block shuffle order; early stopping selects the best epoch solely by Codeforces Div. 2 Dev within-position accuracy (earliest epoch on ties). Both `A0` and `A1` are replicated across three random seeds (42, 101, 202) to quantify training variability (internal training-split hyperparameter verification and full epoch logs appear in Supplement S2).
4. **External Generative Judge (`DeepSeek-Flash`):** We prompted `DeepSeek-Flash` (with thinking disabled, temperature 0, and structured JSON output) to assign each anonymized statement and its constraints an integer difficulty score from 0 to 100 using three scale anchors (0, 50, and 100). Exact predicted ties receive 0.5 credit in standalone accuracy evaluation; the verbatim prompt, server model identifier, repeatability check, and 153-pair `AB/BA` pairwise-prompting diagnostic appear in Supplement S4.

### 2.4 Chronological Fixed-Review-Budget Decision Simulation

To evaluate operational triage without hindsight leakage, we run a **retrospective decision simulation** over historical same-position requests ordered chronologically by contest-pair block. Letting $N_t$ denote the cumulative number of raw within-position requests (including official rating ties) arriving through block $t$ and $b \in \{0\%, 10\%, 20\%, 30\%, 50\%\}$ denote the fixed manual review budget, the online block referral quota is $\Delta Q_t = \lfloor b N_t \rfloor - \lfloor b N_{t-1} \rfloor$. Inside block $t$, each strategy sorts requests by ascending predicted score margin $|s_a - s_b|$ (breaking exact margin ties deterministically via a hashed pair identifier, checked across five independent salts) and refers the smallest-margin $\Delta Q_t$ pairs to manual review. Referred pairs are counted as handled by the maintainer; the remaining automated pairs are evaluated against platform-published difficulty, using Joint Ridge as an operational tie-breaker whenever a discrete ranker predicts an exact tie on an automated pair.

Our primary operational metric is **verifiable automatic misorderings per 100 raw within-position requests**, with unverifiable official rating ties reported alongside as a separate disposition column. To separate ranking quality from referral selection cleanly, we conduct two complementary analyses on the exact same requests and block quotas: (i) a 2×2 **Who Refers × Who Ranks** counterfactual matrix, and (ii) a **same-ranker margin-versus-random referral** comparison holding the automated ranker fixed and computing the paired block-bootstrap reduction in automated errors relative to exact expected intra-block uniform random referral.

For full timeline transparency, the 30% review scenario and baseline AtCoder ABC counts (213, 141, and 119 automated errors) were observed during earlier historical exploration prior to our formal analysis freeze; all Qwen3 factorial models were trained and evaluated afterward under the frozen replay specification, and a separate prospective protocol for future AtCoder Beginner Contest rounds was frozen on 21 November 2026. All contests analyzed here preceded that freeze, so we treat 30% as the fixed primary scenario on this historical sample. Because no live human maintainer trials were conducted, all workflow metrics represent offline retrospective decision simulation rather than measured editorial time or human-corrected accuracy.

Three bounded findings emerge from the controlled four-cell Qwen3 factorial and external judge comparison (Figure 2):

1. **Adding a training-only position offset (`A1 − A0`) yielded no stable within-position gain.** On the primary in-domain comparison (Codeforces Div. 2), `A1 − A0` on seed 42 was **−1.09 percentage points** (95% contest-pair block CI −6.09 to +4.03), and the three training seeds spanned **−1.09, +1.09, and +1.46 points** (mean `+0.49 ± 1.38` pp). On Codeforces Other Divisions, `A1 − A0` was **−1.76 points** (−7.63 to +3.65; three seeds `−1.76, +0.00, +2.20`, mean `+0.15 ± 1.99` pp). On cross-platform AtCoder ABC, seed 42 showed `A1 − A0 =` **−4.80 points** (−7.86 to −1.84), yet across all three training seeds the difference was **−4.80, −2.48, and +0.93 points** (mean `−2.12 ± 2.88` pp), reversing sign on one of three seeds. Because the block-bootstrap CI conditions on one trained checkpoint, seed-to-seed variance substantially widens practical uncertainty. Training logs (Supplement S2) show three observed correlates in `A1`: the learned offsets $b_k$ rapidly absorbed cross-slot separation ($b_E$ reached `+0.53`–`+0.97` relative to $b_A = 0$), different-slot training loss fell lower than in `A0`, and early stopping triggered at epoch 1 on two of three seeds. Whether reduced cross-slot gradient pressure on $f(x)$ directly caused lower ABC point estimates on two seeds remains an **untested hypothesis** rather than an identified causal effect; empirically, the training-only position offset offered no reliable benefit over plain LoRA.
2. **Un-offset LoRA (`A0`) delivered a repeatable cross-platform gain on AtCoder ABC, whereas frozen probing (`F0`) remained competitive in-domain.** Relative to frozen `F0` (`55.88%`), `Qwen3-A0` improved AtCoder ABC within-position accuracy across all three training seeds (`61.61%, 59.13%, 59.13%`; mean `59.96 ± 1.43%`), with primary-seed gains of **+5.73 points** over `F0` (95% CI +1.85 to +9.66) and **+8.51 points** over `Joint Ridge` (+3.83 to +13.18). On Codeforces Div. 2, by contrast, frozen `F0` achieved the highest point estimate (`59.85%`), with `A0 − F0 = −4.74` points (−11.11 to +1.54) and `A0 − Joint = −1.82` points (−8.16 to +4.11).
3. **Strong cross-platform asymmetry persists for `DeepSeek-Flash`, while difficult Codeforces pairs defeat all statement-only models.** On AtCoder ABC, `DeepSeek-Flash` reached **68.73%** (95% CI 65.57 to 71.66), exceeding `Joint Ridge` by **+15.63 points** (+11.92 to +19.58) and local `Qwen3-A0` by **+7.12 points** (+2.38 to +11.74). Yet on both Codeforces cohorts, `DeepSeek-Flash` was statistically indistinguishable from `Joint Ridge` (`−2.55` points, −9.49 to +4.26 on Div. 2; `+1.10` points, −6.14 to +8.52 on Other Divisions). A concrete Div. 2 counterexample illustrates this boundary: comparing Codeforces `2232 F` (official rating 3,000) against `2234 F` (rating 2,000) in adjacent slot F, `Joint Ridge` (1,269 vs. 1,753), `Qwen3-A0` (−2.09 vs. +0.39), and `DeepSeek-Flash` (62 vs. 88) all predict the 2,000-rated problem as markedly harder, showing that concise Codeforces statements can conceal substantial algorithmic difficulty from every evaluated statement-only ranker.

## 4. Results II: Separating Ranking Quality from Referral Selection Under a Fixed Budget

Under the fixed 30% chronological manual review budget, whole-policy performance separates sharply across platforms (Figure 3 and Table 3). On **AtCoder ABC** (646 raw requests: 193 referred, 453 automated, 0 official ties), `Joint Ridge` left **213 verifiable automatic misorderings** (`32.97` per 100 requests; `47.02%` automated error rate), essentially matching uniform random referral (`212.1` expected errors). Local **`Qwen3-A0`** reduced automated misorderings to **168** on seed 42 (`26.01` per 100, saving **6.97** errors per 100; 95% CI 3.27 to 10.82), with all three training seeds staying well below `Joint Ridge` (`168, 170, 180` errors; Supplement S7). **`DeepSeek-Flash`** reduced automated misorderings further to **119** (`18.42` per 100 requests), saving **14.55** errors per 100 requests (95% CI 10.46 to 18.57, or 94 fewer misordered pairs at identical manual workload). Even after that reduction, **26.27%** of the 453 automated ABC pairs remained misordered, documenting both the gain and the residual error burden. On **Codeforces Div. 2** (400 requests: 120 referred, 280 automated) and **Codeforces Other Divisions** (341 requests: 102 referred, 239 automated), neither `Qwen3-A0` nor `DeepSeek-Flash` reduced automated errors reliably relative to `Joint Ridge` (`82` vs. `90` and `87` on Div. 2, alongside `80–93` unverifiable official ties; `73` vs. `75` and `65` on Other Divisions; full model rows including `PW-Joint`, `F0`, `F1`, and `A1` appear in Supplement S7).

## 5. Discussion and Conclusion

Three bounded conclusions follow from these retrospective experiments. First, under a fixed manual review budget, **ranking capability and referral selection must be evaluated separately**. On the historical AtCoder ABC workload (646 same-position requests, 193 referred), switching from `Joint Ridge` to `DeepSeek-Flash` reduced automated misorderings by 14.55 per 100 requests (213 to 119), with both stronger automated ranking (`213 -> 141` under ridge referral) and fixed-ranker margin referral (`+3.67` saved per 100 over random referral's `142.7`) contributing verifiable gains; meanwhile, locally adapted `Qwen3-A0` cut whole-policy errors cleanly across all three seeds (`168, 170, 180` vs. `213`) with zero API fees, but its margin-referral increment over random selection (`+0.78`, `−1.13` to `+2.61`) remained uncertain. Even under the strongest policy on ABC, `26.27%` of automated pairs remained misordered, quantifying the residual error burden that maintainers still face.

Second, in our controlled four-cell `Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B` adaptation study, adding a *training-only position offset* (`A1 − A0`) failed to produce a stable within-position gain (`−1.09` pp, `−6.09` to `+4.03` on Codeforces Div. 2; three seeds `−1.09, +1.09, +1.46`; three seeds on ABC `−4.80, −2.48, +0.93` with one sign reversal). By contrast, un-offset LoRA (`A0`) consistently improved cross-platform ABC accuracy over frozen probing (`F0`, `55.88%`) across all three seeds (`61.61%, 59.13%, 59.13%`), while frozen probing remained competitive on in-domain Codeforces Div. 2 (`59.85%`).

Third, platform and methodological boundaries strictly limit how far these results generalize. On both historical Codeforces cohorts, neither whole-policy error reduction nor fixed-ranker margin referral over random selection was established, and a behavioral solve-rate check confirmed that this null separation is not merely an artifact of Codeforces rating discretization: official ratings and in-contest solve rates agreed on **262 of 274** Div. 2 pairs (`95.62%`, 95% CI `93.01%–97.93%`, rising to `189/190` or `99.47%` above a `0.03` solve-rate gap), and replaying the 30% review budget against solve-rate order reproduced the same null separation (Supplement S6). Additional scope bounds include the small post-cutoff ABC pilot (3 blocks, 21 pairs: `DeepSeek-Flash` `76.19%`, `Qwen3-A0` `66.67%`), the auxiliary AtCoder ARC cohort where local models remained near `48%–54%`, and the absence of post-freeze contests after 21 November 2026 (Supplement S5). Furthermore, because historical contest statements are publicly archived, model release dates alone cannot rule out prior exposure in foundation-model pretraining data, bounding our findings as retrospective. Finally, because no live human editorial trials were performed, we do not claim measured gains in maintainer time or post-review accuracy; deploying this triage policy on any other problem bank requires directly evaluating local request structure and acceptable error tolerance rather than extrapolating unmeasured platform mechanisms from ABC and Codeforces.

**Data and code availability.** The accompanying anonymous replication package separates three reproducibility tiers, documented in its README: (i) *bundled directly* are zero-prose item-level scores, derived surface features, frozen specifications, and reference tables; (ii) *recomputed directly* by running the bundled verification entry script on the item scores are Table 2 within-position and pooled accuracies with contest-pair block intervals, the three-seed `A0/A1` table, Table 3 and Table 4a 30%-budget counts and 2×2 swaps, Table 4b same-ranker margin-versus-random referral gains with block intervals, and the 1,292-item AtCoder ABC cost breakdown; and (iii) *retraining from raw statements* requires retrieving public Codeforces and AtCoder statements by archived problem identifier, preprocessing with the included inequality-preserving parser, and loading pinned open weights (`Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B`, `google-bert/bert-base-uncased`, `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5`) via the included reference training script.
